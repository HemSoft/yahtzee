# Desktop and web replacement direction

The owner delegated the visual choice and confirmed desktop/web scope. Native mobile is excluded. Existing scoring, guest lifecycle, and backend authority stay intact.

The current product combines variable dice counts with a shared scorecard and instant legal-score previews. The actual audience is one owner playing for themselves; no broader cultural preference is assumed. The first screen must make rolling, holding, and choosing a score feel like playing a game. Avoid both the existing spreadsheet and its predictable casino-table opposite.

## Grounded candidates

1. A contemporary handheld game console: a dedicated play area and adjacent persistent score display.
2. Live sports scorekeeping: a clear turn indicator, decisive score changes, and compact standings.
3. A contemporary board-game rulebook: legible grouped scoring and visible rule explanations.
4. A bowling scoring terminal: persistent players and immediate input feedback, with a risk of repeating the spreadsheet.
5. A modern music sampler: tactile pads, grouped controls, a stable transport area, and an adjacent sequence of decisions.
6. A museum's hands-on probability exhibit: physical-feeling dice with explanations revealed at the decision point.
7. A competition timer: deliberate typography and an action-first layout, with a risk of too little game character.

## Selected direction

Candidate 5, seed c28b6587, Operate. Adapt the modern sampler's grouped input pads and persistent action area, not its musical terminology. Mineral-white shell, deep evergreen rolling deck, porcelain dice with actual pip geometry, and a clay-orange roll control. Graphite-green dark appearance is equally supported. Use a self-hosted Manrope face for the compact console lettering and readable tabular scores. No audio, invented controls, glow, or retro hardware ornament.

The owner said "you choose", so choose the assigned direction without asking them to operate another preference picker. Build directly in code; no generated image is needed for dice geometry or controls.

## Challenger judgments

All judgments compare identification with the owner's requested modern personal game and clarity of the roll/hold/score task.

- Miura fold: declined on both. Expanding crease graphs obscure ordinary scoring; keep its discipline of progressive spatial disclosure through responsive panel stacking.
- Cloud quarry: declined on both. Weather construction is unrelated to dice decisions; keep its decisive division between a substantial play field and clear light space.
- Developer console: competitive on clarity, weaker on identification because the owner rejected a utilitarian spreadsheet. Keep action isolation and explicit pending/error states, not developer aesthetics.
- Factory catalog sleeve: declined on both. Coded labels would hide categories; keep its restraint in visual hierarchy and remove redundant decoration.
- Emission-line rail: declined on both. Tiny legends and line-coded states weaken scoring readability; keep one consistent alignment system for score values.
- Nixie counter: declined on both. Retro tubes conflict with the rejection of old-school styling; keep precise, stable numeral placement during score changes.

## Risks

Dense six-dice categories must remain usable at the existing desktop window size. A split scorecard preserves all categories while avoiding microscopic rows. The play area must wrap up to 20 dice without horizontal page overflow. Preserve keyboard scoring, retry, quit during pending requests, local preferences, and both appearances.
